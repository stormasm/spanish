In Spanish, **"te acuerdas"** is correct because the verb meaning *"to remember"* is **acordarse** (a pronominal verb that always requires a reflexive pronoun like *te*), whereas **recordar** (without a reflexive pronoun) means *"to remember"* or *"to remind"* and doesn't take *te* when used in that way.

### **How the Two Verbs Work**

* **Acordarse (de algo)** — Pronominal verb meaning *"to remember"* or *"to recall."* Because it is pronominal, it pairs with reflexive pronouns (*me, te, se, nos, os, se*).  
  * *¿**Te acuerdas** de su nombre?* ("Do you remember his/her name?")  
  * *No **me acuerdo** bien.* ("I don't remember well.")  
* **Recordar (algo)** — Non-reflexive transitive verb meaning *"to remember"* or *"to recall"* something directly, without needing *te*.  
  * *¿**Recuerdas** su nombre?* ("Do you remember his/her name?")  
  * *No **recuerdo** bien.* ("I don't remember well.")

### **Why "Te recuerdas" Sounds Wrong**

Saying **"te recuerdas"** mixes the two patterns together: it adds the reflexive pronoun *te* from *acordarse* onto the conjugated form of *recordar*. Native speakers will immediately understand what you mean, so it won't block communication, but it sounds unnatural and ungrammatical in standard Spanish.  
*(Note: **Recordarse** does exist in some regional varieties or specific contexts—such as "se recuerda que..." in formal announcements—but **"te recuerdas"** is not standard usage for "do you remember.")*

### **Quick Comparison**

| Meaning | Standard Form | Why It Works |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| "Do you remember?" (with *te*) | **¿Te acuerdas?** *(or ¿Te acuerdas de...?)* | *Acordarse* takes a reflexive pronoun (*te*). |
| "Do you remember?" (no *te*) | **¿Recuerdas?** | *Recordar* acts directly on what is remembered without *te*. |
| "Remind me..." | **Recuérdame...** | *Recordar* \+ indirect object pronoun (*me*) \= "to remind." |
