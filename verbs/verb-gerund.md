Spanish uses several verbs in **verbal periphrases** (*perífrasis verbales*) alongside a gerund (-ando/-iendo) to express duration, continuity, or progression—each adding a distinct nuance about how the action unfolds over time.

| Verb Construction | Core Nuance | Example | English Equivalent |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **llevar \+ gerund** | Measures **accumulated time** up to the present (requires a time expression). | *Llevo dos horas estudiando.* | I've been studying for two hours. |
| **seguir / continuar \+ gerund** | Emphasizes **continuation** without interruption ("still"). | *Sigue lloviendo.* | It's still raining. |
| **ir \+ gerund** | Emphasizes **gradual progression** or step-by-step accumulation. | *Voy entendiendo la gramática.* | I'm gradually understanding the grammar. |
| **venir \+ gerund** | Highlights an ongoing action that **started in the past and leads up to now** (often with trends or repeated habits). | *Vengo diciendo esto desde hace meses.* | I've been saying this for months. |
| **andar \+ gerund** | Conveys an **intermittent, casual, or ongoing** activity happening around the current period. | *Anda buscando trabajo.* | He/she is going around looking for a job. |
| **pasarse \+ time \+ gerund** | Emphasizes spending a **long or excessive amount of time** on an action. | *Se pasó toda la tarde leyendo.* | He/she spent the whole afternoon reading. |

### **Key Structural Differences**

* **Requirement for a time expression:** **Llevar** and **pasarse** require an explicit unit of time (*25 minutos*, *toda la tarde*, *tres años*). If you drop the time expression, the meaning changes completely (*Llevo caminando* alone sounds incomplete, whereas *Sigo caminando* or *Voy caminando* are complete sentences).  
* **Negation:** **Llevar** is the only one that cleanly takes **sin \+ infinitive** to measure the duration of something *not* happening (*Llevo tres días sin dormir* — "I haven't slept in three days"). With **seguir**, you use **sin \+ infinitive** to mean "still not" (*Sigue sin llover* — "It's still not raining").  
* **Word order flexibility:** Just like *llevar*, **pasarse** allows you to place the time block either before or after the gerund (*Me pasé dos horas esperando* or *Me pasé esperando dos horas*), though placing the time first is far more common.

### **Using *Hacer* and *Desde* (Alternative Structures)**

When you want to express duration **without using a gerund**, Spanish switches to two standard formulas with the simple present tense:

> 1. **Hacer \+ \[time\] \+ que \+ \[present tense verb\]:** Focuses on the elapsed time as a standalone fact (*Hace 25 minutos que camino*).  
> 2. **\[Present tense verb\] \+ desde hace \+ \[time\]:** Focuses on the action first, attaching the duration at the end (*Camino desde hace 25 minutos*).
