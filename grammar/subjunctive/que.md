# **Why Olvidar Uses the Subjunctive**

[https\://gemini.google.com/app/b2b4d2534d1479c4](https://gemini.google.com/app/b2b4d2534d1479c4)

*User prompt: why is the phrase que no se te olvide why is the verb olvidar in the subjunctive*

Response: The verb **olvidar** is in the subjunctive because **que no se te olvide** is a shortened **indirect command** (also called a *jussive subjunctive* or *que \+ subjunctive* command).

### **1\. The Elliptical Structure (The Missing Main Clause)**

When a Spanish sentence starts with **que \+ subjunctive**, there is an implied main verb of desire, warning, or insistence that has been dropped in everyday speech:

> * *(Quiero / Te digo / Ten cuidado de) **que no se te olvide**.*  
> * *(I want / I'm telling you / Be careful) **that it not be forgotten by you**.*

Because the hidden main clause expresses **will, influence, or a directive**, the dependent clause introduced by *que* triggers the subjunctive—just like any standard WEIRDO (Wishes, Emotions, Impersonal expressions, Recommendations, Doubt/Denial, Ojalá) construction.

### **2\. Why Not Use a Direct Negative Command?**

You *could* say **"No lo olvides"** (direct negative *tú* command, which also uses the subjunctive form), so why do native speakers strongly prefer **"Que no se te olvide"**?

> * **The "Accidental *Se*" (*Se* involuntario):** Using *se \+ indirect object (te)* frames the forgetting as something that *happens to you* rather than an intentional action you choose to do. Literally, *"may it not slip your mind."* This softens the warning and removes blame.  
> * **Conversational Naturalness:** Starting with *que* acts as a gentle reminder or emphatic nudge ("Don't forget\! / Make sure you don't forget\!"), which sounds warmer and more idiomatic than a direct command.

### **Common Parallels**

Spanish uses this exact *que \+ subjunctive* pattern constantly for wishes, warnings, and indirect directives:

> * **¡Que te vaya bien\!** — *(Espero) que te vaya bien.* (Hope it goes well for you\!)  
> * **¡Que aproveche\!** — *(Deseo) que aproveche.* (Enjoy your meal\!)  
> * **¡Que no llueva\!** — *(Ojalá) que no llueva.* (Let's hope it doesn't rain\!)  
> * **¡Que no se te haga tarde\!** — *(Ten cuidado de) que no se te haga tarde.* (Don't run late\!)
